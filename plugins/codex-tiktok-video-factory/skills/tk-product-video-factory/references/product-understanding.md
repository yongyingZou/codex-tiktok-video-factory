# Product understanding gate

Do not move from inventory directly to video directions. Establish what the product is and why a
viewer might buy it before treating source clips as editing material.

## Required product model

Produce these fields from `product.md`, every product image, every source video's visuals, original
speech, and hard subtitles:

- `literal_category`: the plain product/category identity a shopper would search for;
- `target_users`: people for whom the product is visibly or explicitly relevant;
- `primary_need`: the strongest underlying problem or desire;
- `solution_mechanism`: how the product addresses that need;
- `use_scenes`: common and niche situations, with evidence level;
- `objections`: reasons a viewer may hesitate, such as heat, discomfort, stability, complexity, or
  appearance;
- `primary_benefits`: benefits capable of supporting a complete purchase argument;
- `supporting_benefits`: useful reassurance that normally belongs inside another direction;
- `variants`: sizes, colors, quantities, bundles, and promotions;
- `claim_limits`: medical, absolute, unsupported, or otherwise unsafe wording;
- `unknowns`: facts the supplied evidence cannot establish.

The folder suffix is navigation help only. It is never evidence for `literal_category`.

## Evidence synthesis

Use product images to establish product identity, construction, variants, and seller-presented
facts. Use source videos to learn how creators explain the product, what actions and scenes they
show, what objections they answer, and how their narratives are structured. Transcribe meaningful
speech and interpret hard subtitles; visual-only review is incomplete when language carries sales
meaning.

Source repetition increases confidence but does not convert a claim into a scientifically proven
fact. Record evidence as `confirmed_user`, `product_source`, `repeated_source`, `single_source`,
`inference`, or `blocked`.

External research may broaden category understanding, terminology, common use scenes, and likely
objections. It must not overwrite the supplied product's actual construction, specification, or
seller-confirmed facts. Label researched context separately from product evidence.

## Value hierarchy test

Before proposing a direction, ask:

1. Would a viewer understand what the product is without already knowing it?
2. Does this direction contain a meaningful problem, desire, or purchase motivation?
3. Does the product visibly or credibly address it?
4. Do available clips support the story rather than merely illustrate isolated features?
5. Is the direction materially different in audience, problem, purchase reason, and supporting
   shots from the rest of the batch?

Water resistance, washability, color choice, packaging quantity, and similar information are often
supporting benefits or variants. Promote one to an independent direction only when the product,
audience, supplied evidence, and material coverage make it a complete buying argument.

## Analysis completion gate

Do not claim product analysis is complete unless every input has an explicit status and the product
model above is populated or marked unknown. Report contradictions and missing evidence immediately.
Do not hide uncertainty by writing generic copy.
